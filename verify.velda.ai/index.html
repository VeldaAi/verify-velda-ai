This is the screen Google reviews. Four things on it are ours to get right.

First, the important bit

Google runs three different checks.

They are not the same thing, and they cost very different amounts of time. Most apps need the first one — that is what our prices cover. Read them in order.

01
Our prices cover this Brand check The name, logo, homepage and privacy link people see when they sign in. Often automatic, and a person usually takes 2–3 business days. This is what $100 and $249 pay for.
02
Not in our prices Sensitive-scope check Needed if you read Calendar, send Gmail, or similar. Google asks for a demo video. Sign-in alone does not need it. Drive’s drive.file scope — the files a person picks — is not sensitive, and does not by itself need the video. Not every use of Drive is the same.
03
A separate thing again Restricted-scope check Broad access — all of Gmail, all of Drive. Google’s security-assessment page says an app asking for restricted scopes must do an annual assessment (CASA). Google does not charge that fee; if an outside assessor does it, the assessor does. Some tiers are a self-scan. The assessment is not included in $100, $149 or $249, and we never sign you up for it.

Google’s own timing notes, which are not promises: the brand check is often automatic within minutes; up to 10 days for a sensitive review; about 6 weeks for a restricted review. Some apps sit outside verification entirely — personal use, testing, or an internal Workspace app. Sources are on the done-for-you page.
